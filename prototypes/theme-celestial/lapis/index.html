Lapis & Gold Leaf

An illuminated book of hours, read at night. The ground is ultramarine as it comes from real lapis: deep, a little uneven, with flecks of pyrite and pale calcite veins under the surface. Gold is laid as leaf on a raised gesso bed, burnished to a bright edge and worn at the corners. It is the warmest and most ornamental of the five, so it keeps one illuminated thing per screen (a border band, a decorated initial, a drawing) and lets everything else stay quiet type on stone.

Borrows from Leather & Ink: the emboss and deboss recipes, the stitched card (now a gilt-filleted panel), italic gold kickers, oldstyle book furniture, the soft-ink drawing style, one bold thing per page, and the rose-for-interaction, gold-for-ornament rule.

On the iPhone

Native structure kept: large titles, a floating iOS 26 tab bar with Search on its own, glass toolbar buttons, plain lists with hairline rules. Each screen has one illuminated thing.

Up close

How each surface is made, and what it would cost to draw in SwiftUI. The rule of thumb: anything per-pixel (noise, flecks, specular gold) is baked once into an image or a shader; gradients and solid shapes are drawn live.

The lapis ground

Built up in four layers, left to right: the ultramarine base with a cool lamp at the top left, then calcite veins, then pyrite flecks, then fine noise in overlay. The reader drops back to the first layer only, darker.

reader ground
moderate once, cheap afterBase and lamp are two gradients (cheap, as in LeatherBackground). Veins, flecks and noise should be one baked tile (a 512 pt @2x CGImage made at launch, like Grain.tile, or an asset), drawn with resizable(resizingMode: .tile). Never blend noise live with .blendMode(.overlay) over scrolling content. Draw the ground once behind the whole window, not per screen.

Gold leaf, three ways

Left: a flat leaf gradient. Middle: the same with a gesso edge (an inner highlight above, an inner shade below, a contact shadow). Right: full leaf with a specular bump lit from the top left, grain in the metal and crackle at the worn edges.

moderateGesso edge: gradient plus a 1 pt inner highlight and shade (.fill(.shadow(.inner))) and one small drop shadow. Fine for a handful of buttons; avoid on every list row.
costly liveSpecular bump, grain and crackle: SVG-style lighting filters have no SwiftUI equivalent. Either pre-render each ornament (initials A–Z, rosette, band tile) as an image asset, or write one Metal .layerEffect shader (iOS 17+) that does the bump and grain on a masked shape. Use the shader only on still, small ornaments, never while scrolling a long list.

Star motifs

Rosette star (eight points, from manuscript borders), quatrefoil, the four-point sparkle, and moon phases for anything that waxes.

cheapAll are single Shape paths filled with a gradient. Draw them as SwiftUI Paths or vector template assets; the rosette inside a row is a 11 pt symbol, practically free.

Border band

Gilt fillets top and bottom, gold lozenges between pale dots: the strip under a page title. One per screen, never inside rows.

cheapBake one 28 × 16 pt tile with the leaf already lit, and tile it horizontally with Image(...).resizable(resizingMode: .tile). Two 1.5 pt gradient rectangles for the fillets.

Glass chrome

The tab bar and toolbar buttons stay system glass, tinted lapis, with a gold hairline only on the selected tab.

system costNative glassEffect / bar materials: the system already pays for the blur, so a tint adds nothing. Don’t rebuild the bars to add gilt; a custom blurred bar over a tiled texture is the costliest thing on this page.

Decorated initial

Gold-leaf frame, a lapis field with cream filigree, a gilt Bodoni letter. One per chapter and one on the summary.

moderatePre-render 26 initials (plus a fallback) as image assets at 3 sizes, or draw the frame as a baked image and the letter as live Text with a gradient foregroundStyle. In the reader it sits in a text flow, which SwiftUI can’t float: lay out the first paragraph as an HStack of initial plus text, or use a TextKit view.
